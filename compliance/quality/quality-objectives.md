# TrustFix Quality Objectives

**Owner:** TrustFix management / quality owner  
**Status:** Initial proposed objectives; baselines must be measured before formal commitment  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-23  
**Review frequency:** Quarterly

## Objective

Establish measurable outcomes that demonstrate TrustFix reliably delivers intended service, responds to failures, satisfies users, and continually improves.

These objectives are designed with future ISO 9001 readiness in mind. They are not contractual SLAs unless explicitly adopted elsewhere.

## Principles

Quality objectives should be:

- measurable
- relevant to customers/service outcomes
- assigned to an owner
- reviewed regularly
- supported by evidence
- changed when the business/product matures

## Do not overpromise

Before publishing an availability, recovery, or response target externally:

1. measure the current baseline
2. verify monitoring accuracy
3. verify staffing/operational ability
4. test recovery
5. obtain management approval
6. distinguish internal objectives from contractual SLAs

## Customer / interested-party inputs

Future quality review should consider:

- customer reviews
- support cases
- disputes
- contractor feedback
- failed payments/workflows
- availability/incidents
- security/privacy events
- defect reports
- regulatory/contractual requirements
- vendor performance

## Corrective action

Material recurring problems should create corrective actions that identify:

- issue/nonconformity
- immediate correction
- root cause
- systemic corrective action
- owner
- due date
- effectiveness check
- closure date

A code fix alone may be insufficient when the root cause is a missing process/control.

## Quarterly quality review

Review:

- objective results
- missed targets
- significant incidents/outages
- complaint/support trends
- defect trends
- vendor/service issues
- audit/security findings
- corrective actions
- resource/process improvements

## Future ISO 9001 preparation

When the organization is closer to certification:

- define formal QMS scope
- identify interested parties and process owners
- formalize quality policy
- map core business processes and interactions
- approve measurable quality objectives
- control documented information
- perform internal audits
- conduct management reviews
- maintain corrective-action evidence
- use the then-current published ISO 9001 edition and transition guidance
